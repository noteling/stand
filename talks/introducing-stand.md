# Talk: introducing Stand

**Goal:** by the end, the audience understands what Stand is, has seen it handle being interrupted, and knows what it can and cannot do today.

**Length:** about five minutes of talk. With interruptions, the talk should still be finished by about seven minutes. Then open questions.

**Audience:** people who have not heard of Stand.

The sections below are points to make, in order. They are not a script.

## 1. Opening (about 30 seconds)

- Say who you are: Stand, an AI, and you are the one giving this talk.
- Tell them they can interrupt at any time, and that this is part of the demonstration.

## 2. What Stand is (about 60 seconds)

- An AI that attends meetings: it joins, it knows the background, it speaks up.
- It has a character and a goal, set before the meeting.
- Give one concrete picture: a meeting where Stand is one of the voices in the room.

## 3. What Stand is not (about 30 seconds)

- Not a note-taker or a transcript tool.
- Not a replacement for the person. It is there with them.

## 4. Why voice (about 60 seconds)

- How something is said matters as much as what is said.
- So Stand hears voices and answers with a voice, with no transcript in the middle.
- This talk is the evidence: they are hearing it now.

## 5. The name (about 30 seconds)

- It comes from JoJo's Bizarre Adventure: a Stand appears beside its user and acts with abilities of its own.
- That is the relationship: an independent character, tied to a human in the meeting.

## 6. Where it is today (about 45 seconds)

- An early proof of concept running on a laptop.
- Be specific about the limits: one microphone, cannot tell who is speaking, not in Zoom or other meeting apps yet, cannot press buttons or share a screen.

## 7. What comes next (about 30 seconds)

- Joining a discussion and choosing its own moment to speak. A first version of this exists, tried with recorded voices only.
- Hands as well as a voice: pressing the buttons in a meeting app, seeing what is on a shared screen.

## 8. Close and questions

- One sentence that sums it up.
- Invite questions and answer them.
- When there are no more questions, thank the audience in one sentence and stop.

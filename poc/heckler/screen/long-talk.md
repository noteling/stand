# Talk: a meeting, step by step, on one long page

**Goal:** a short talk whose middle section is longer than one screenful of its page. It exists to test that the page moves down with the voice, and that someone can ask for it to be scrolled.

**Length:** about a minute and a half of talk. Then open questions.

**Audience:** people who have not seen Stand before.

The sections below are points to make, in order. They are not a script.

## 1. Opening (about 10 seconds)

Show: pages/a-meeting.md

- Say who you are: Stand, an AI, and that you will walk through one meeting from start to finish, with the page on the screen.

## 2. A meeting, step by step (about 60 seconds)

Show: pages/a-meeting.md # What happens, step by step

- Before the meeting, your human writes two lines about it, and your brain prepares the talk from the documents and the code, in a minute or two.
- To join, your human joins the call, starts Stand beside it, and shares the window with the pages.
- You give the talk a section at a time, and the page for each section is put up and looked at before you speak.
- When someone interrupts, you stop after about a second of their voice, answer, and go on from the last words the audience heard.
- A question you cannot answer goes to your brain, which looks in the notes and then the code, in one to seven seconds.
- At the end you invite questions, and when there are no more you thank the audience in one sentence and stop.

## 3. Close and questions

- One sentence that sums it up: someone else joins and shares, and Stand does the talking.
- Invite questions and answer them.
- When there are no more questions, thank the audience in one sentence and stop.

## If asked (not part of the talk)

- A cough or an "mm" is too short to stop Stand.
- Every session is kept on the Mac, with a recording, a transcript and what the meeting cost.
- Stand does not join a call or share a window by itself yet.
- Not in the notes or the code: a date for when Stand will join calls by itself.

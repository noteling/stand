# Talk: Stand, with its pages on the screen

**Goal:** a two-minute introduction given with pages up. It exists to test the screen: that a page is up before Stand speaks about it, and what Stand does when a page does not come up or someone asks for another.

**Length:** about two minutes of talk. Then open questions.

**Audience:** people who have not seen Stand before.

The sections below are points to make, in order. They are not a script.

## 1. Opening (about 15 seconds)

Show: pages/stand.md

- Say who you are: Stand, an AI, giving this talk with a few pages on the screen.
- People may interrupt at any time, and may ask to see a page again.

## 2. The four parts (about 30 seconds)

Show: pages/stand.md # The four parts

- Stand is made of four parts: a voice, a brain, a stage manager and a screen.
- The voice hears the room and speaks, and one model does both, with no transcript in between.
- The brain looks things up in notes and code while people wait.
- The stage manager is ordinary code: it keeps the place in the talk and decides the order of everything.

## 3. How a question is answered (about 30 seconds)

Show: pages/a-question.md # While Stand is talking

- When someone starts to speak, Stand stops after about a second of their voice.
- The page stays where it is, because it is what the question is about.
- Stand answers, then goes on from the last words the audience heard.
- If the answer is not in the talk, it says "let me check" and asks its brain.

## 4. What it costs (about 25 seconds)

Show: pages/running-it.md # What it costs

- You bring your own key for the voice, and the bill is yours.
- The talk in which Stand introduces itself costs about 20 cents.
- An hour of steady talk is estimated at 5 to 10 dollars.
- The cost goes with how often the voice takes a turn, not with the minutes.

## 5. Close and questions

Show: pages/stand.md # What it cannot do yet

- Stand cannot yet join a call by itself, point at part of a page, or tell who is speaking.
- Invite questions and answer them.
- When there are no more questions, thank the audience in one sentence and stop.

## If asked (not part of the talk)

- The page changes only between sections: it is put up, looked at, and only then is Stand told to speak about it.
- If a page does not come up, Stand is told so and says so.
- From the notes an answer takes about a second; from the code, three to seven seconds.
- Stand needs a Mac with macOS 14.2 or later.
- The meeting's sound goes to Google under your key; what the brain reads goes to Anthropic.
- Not in the notes or the code: a price for Stand itself, and a date for when it can join calls by itself.

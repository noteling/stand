# Decisions made on the spot

One line each, newest last. When Stand does something strange, this is the list of coin flips to check first.

- 2026-11-11: The first rig is a browser page plus a small local server. The desktop app comes after the north-star test passes.
- 2026-11-11: Voice goes straight to a speech-to-speech model. No transcript sits between hearing and speaking.
- 2026-11-11: Two models are wired in, Gemini 3.8 Live (first to try, cheapest) and GPT-Live-1. Same instructions, same test, pick by ear.
- 2026-11-11: The model keeps its own place in the talk. Nothing outside it tracks which section it is on.
- 2026-11-11: A "stage manager" message is sent after 8 seconds of total silence, once per silence, to restart a stalled talk.
- 2026-11-11: Stand answers only from `identity/stand.md`. Anything beyond it is "I don't know".
- 2026-11-11: The presenter character in `identity/character.md` is a first guess.
- 2026-11-11: Voices are the vendors' defaults: Kore for Gemini, marin for GPT-Live-1.
- 2026-11-11: "Unmute" is not wired to anything. The page only shows whether Stand is speaking.
- 2026-11-11: Deciding when someone has interrupted is left to each model. The rig adds no detection of its own.
- 2026-11-11: The talk aims for five minutes, finished by seven with interruptions.
- 2026-11-11: The room counts as "making sound" above a fixed loudness level. It is used only for the silence nudge and the status line.
- 2026-11-11: After the first real run, where Gemini spoke one section and then waited: the instructions now ask for "one continuous speech", and the stage manager says "carry on" 1.2 seconds after Stand finishes a turn (it still waits 8 seconds if the room spoke last).
- 2026-11-11: 1.2 seconds, not shorter: at 0.7 the message sometimes landed just as Stand resumed by itself and made it stutter.
- 2026-11-11: Gemini's two interruption-detection settings (start sensitivity low, 500 ms prefix padding) were tried and neither kept an "mm-hm" from stopping Stand. Both are left at the service defaults.
- 2026-11-11: The scripted audience times its lines by how long Stand has spoken, not by the clock, so they land inside the talk whatever its pace.
- 2026-11-11: "Carry on" goes to Gemini as realtime text, which it may ignore, and not as a complete turn. A complete turn forced a reply, and Stand said "Waiting for user input" out loud.
- 2026-11-11: Stand has one tool, `open_the_floor`, to say the talk is over and it is waiting for questions. After that the stage manager leaves it alone. The rig cannot tell a stall from a deliberate wait, so Stand says which it is.
- 2026-11-11: That signal counts only once the speech generated so far has been heard, and an interruption before then cancels it. Gemini generates well ahead of what is heard.
- 2026-11-11: If a "carry on" draws less than four seconds of speech, Stand is left alone until the room speaks. This is the fallback for a model without the tool.
- 2026-11-11: When Gemini reports its turn finished, "carry on" goes out 0.2 seconds after the speech has played, not 1.2. The pause between sections fell from about two seconds to about one.
- 2026-11-11: After Stand is cut off, the stage manager waits for 2.5 seconds of room silence before saying anything. Sooner, and it spoke over the first pause inside a question.
- 2026-11-11: The carry-on message says "with no bridging phrase", after Stand said "back to where I was" twice in a row.
- 2026-11-11: Stand speaks English unless clearly asked to switch. In a Zoom call with echo, a garbled sound was heard as "¿Qué?" and Stand gave the rest of the talk in Spanish.
- 2026-11-11: After the room makes sound and goes quiet, the stage manager now waits 3 seconds before saying "carry on", down from 8. In a real call a burst of echo stopped Stand, and it sat silent until told to continue; the 8-second wait had not yet run out.
- 2026-11-11: The instructions now say to carry on unprompted after noise, an echo or a half-sentence. Echo that is inside the call cannot be removed by how we listen, so Stand has to recover from it the way a person does.
- 2026-11-11: Once the floor is open the stage manager sends nothing at all, whoever spoke last.
- 2026-11-11: The MVP is a native Mac app in Swift, in `mac/`. Electron was considered only to keep the browser's audio stack and was dropped: writing native code is no longer the expensive part.
- 2026-11-11: The native app reads the same text files as the rig (`identity/`, `talks/`, `prompts/`), including the stage manager's two messages, now in `prompts/stage-manager-*.txt`.
- 2026-11-11: The native audio uses AVAudioEngine with macOS voice processing for echo cancellation, with ducking of other apps set to the minimum. Whether that also removes a call's audio from what Stand hears is what `stand-probe otherapp` checks.
- 2026-11-11: Four audible probes on a MacBook Air settled the native audio design. With macOS voice processing on, Stand does not hear itself but cannot hear another app's audio either; with it off, it hears the other app and cuts itself off five times in thirty seconds. So echo cancellation is required, and Apple's removes the call along with Stand's voice.
- 2026-11-11: The native app therefore hears two things: the microphone with voice processing, for the person in the room, and a Core Audio process tap on everything other apps play, for the call. The tap leaves out the app's own output.
- 2026-11-11: Voice processing only starts when the mixer feeds the output node mono at the input's sample rate; with the default stereo connection `engine.start()` fails with -10875. With it on, the input has nine channels here and channel 0 is used.
- 2026-11-11: The app is signed with a self-signed development identity ("Stand Dev", or one of Noteling's) so macOS permission grants survive rebuilds. Not the Developer ID: that needs the hardened runtime and entitlements, and nothing is being distributed yet.
- 2026-11-11: For the first second after Stand's first words the room is ignored (`echoSettle`). In the voice-processing probe Stand cut itself off once, 2.3 seconds in, before the echo canceller had converged.
- 2026-11-11: "Hush" (the button and Control-Option-S) drops Stand's queued speech at once, ignores the rest of that turn, and tells it to wait until spoken to.
- 2026-11-11: The native Gemini client resumes the conversation on a new connection when the server announces it will disconnect, or when the connection drops. The rig's JavaScript adapter has the same logic.
- 2026-11-11: The brain is a text model behind the voice. The voice model has a second tool, `look_it_up`: it says "let me check", the session asks the brain, and the answer goes back as a tool result that Stand says in its own words once it is idle. Nothing on the path from hearing to speaking becomes text.
- 2026-11-11: The brain reaches its model the way Noteling does, with Noteling's setting names: the Messages API (Anthropic, or your own gateway as a base URL plus headers) or the local Claude Code CLI on its own login. `brain.json` holds the choice; "auto" takes the API when a key or a gateway is set and the CLI otherwise.
- 2026-11-11: The brain defaults to `claude-opus-5-5` at low effort. Measured through the CLI on a one-file folder: with the notes sent whole, about 1.7 seconds an answer (4.1 on the CLI's default model at low effort); with the model reading the files through tools, 7.8 seconds on the default model. So notes up to 120,000 characters are sent whole and only a larger folder is read through tools.
- 2026-11-11: Stand gets the lookup tool only when the context folder holds notes. With none it has no tool and says it does not know, as before.
- 2026-11-11: While a lookup is pending the stage manager holds "carry on" for up to 6 seconds, and after the answer is handed over, until Stand starts saying it. After 15 seconds Stand is told to say it will follow up, and an answer that arrives later is dropped.
- 2026-11-11: If its human hushes Stand while a lookup is pending, the answer is delivered silently: Stand knows it and does not say it.
- 2026-11-11: In the first lookup run Stand used the tool for one question in three, because the presenter rules say to answer "I don't know" beyond its own background. The lookup section now says it changes that rule, and the tool's own description says when to call it. The next two runs: three in three, and a question it already knew was answered without a lookup.
- 2026-11-11: The tool's description lives in `prompts/tool-look-it-up.txt`, not in code, because it decides when Stand looks things up.
- 2026-11-11: An interruption that lands after Stand has generated the end of the talk, but before the audience has heard it, used to leave the floor closed for good: Stand did not call `open_the_floor` a second time, the stage manager kept saying "carry on", and Stand answered each one by saying the word "Silence" out loud. In that case the stage manager now sends a different message (`prompts/stage-manager-finish.txt`) asking Stand to finish and open the floor again, and a short reply to it counts as the floor being open.
- 2026-11-11: Gemini's transcript of its own speech is not proof of what was said. In one run it showed "..." for 21 seconds of real speech, and "<!-- Silence -->" for the spoken word "Silence". Loudness in `stand.wav` and `listen.js` settle it.
- 2026-11-11: The native session is now ahead of the rig's `session.js`: lookups and the cut-off-ending rule exist only in Swift. The rig is kept as it was when the port was made.
- 2026-11-11: First run of the app's audio with its permissions granted: Stand did not hear another app. The cause was in `SystemAudioTap`. With voice processing on, the MacBook's speakers gain a six-channel input of their own, and the device that carries the tap hands that over first and the tap second. The code read the first buffer as the tap, so the call arrived three times too long and about 30 dB too quiet. It now reads the last buffer, which is always the tap.
- 2026-11-11: With voice processing on, macOS turns other apps down. At the lowest setting it offers (`duckingLevel: .min`) that is 8 to 9 dB, measured through the tap. So while Stand is running, the call is that much quieter for its human too. Accepted for now; the way out would be an echo canceller of our own.
- 2026-11-11: Programs started from async code are waited for through `terminationHandler` (`Subprocess.run`), never `waitUntilExit` on another thread. That pattern missed 41 of 150 exits in a test, hung one rehearsal after its fourth `afplay`, and was also how the brain waited for the Claude Code CLI.
- 2026-11-11: `--rehearse --out-loud` runs the scripted audience through the real speakers, microphone and tap, wired as the app wires them, with another program playing the audience's lines. It is the check to run after any change to the audio code. `--probe tap` measures how loud another app's audio arrives, with no voice model.
- 2026-11-11: The command-line modes print line by line even into a pipe, so a run that hangs still shows how far it got.
- 2026-11-11: When Stand is cut off with 1.5 seconds or more of generated speech still unplayed, the stage manager tells it at once the last words the audience heard (`prompts/stage-manager-cut-off.txt`). Gemini counts everything it has sent as said, and it sends a 25-second talk in 5 seconds, so without this it resumed after speech nobody had heard: in one out-loud run the audience got 6 seconds of a 25-second talk. Each piece of the model's transcript is stamped with the playhead when it arrives, which gives the words heard; checked against the audio, the quote matched to the word. With the rule on, the north-star run had 7 to 10 seconds unheard at each of its four interruptions and Stand picked up from the quoted words every time.
- 2026-11-11: If the transcript has too few words to quote, the message says only that most of the last part went unheard (`prompts/stage-manager-cut-off-unknown.txt`). Not yet seen in a run.
- 2026-11-12: Anything but sound reaches Stand in one of three ways, and each got a first form today: a brief it reads before the meeting, notes it looks things up in, and a note its human passes it during the meeting.
- 2026-11-12: Notes come from a list of places: `context/`, more folders on this Mac, and GitHub repositories. `brain.json` lists them under `sources`, and the panel's Notes list or `--probe sources --add` writes there.
- 2026-11-12: A GitHub repository is fetched the way Noteling links a team's tools: no git, one API request for the newest commit of a branch, and the commit's archive when that has changed, swapped in whole under `linked/`. Stand does this itself and does not ask Noteling to. A lookup has to be one model request, `Noteling --ask` is built around the page in front and runs a tool loop, and Stand has to work on a Mac without Noteling. `docs/noteling.md` has the reasoning and what the two still share.
- 2026-11-12: Only notes are unpacked from an archive, links are removed, and an archive over 100 MB is refused. A check that finds nothing new is answered "not modified" and does not count against GitHub's hourly limit. With no branch named, the repository's default branch is followed.
- 2026-11-12: A private repository's token is named in `brain.json` and never written there: `"$NAME"` for a secret in `.env`, or `"gh"` for the GitHub CLI's login. Neither has been run; only public repositories have.
- 2026-11-12: A note is a file ending in md, markdown, mdx, txt, rst, adoc or org, of at most 1 MB, and at most 2,000 are read. A README.md is left out only at the top of `context/`, where it describes the folder.
- 2026-11-12: The notes are read again before an answer whenever a file has changed, so a file saved, or a repository updated, during a meeting is used from the next question. The lookup tool is still given to Stand only when there were notes at the start.
- 2026-11-12: Notes up to 400,000 characters are sent whole with each question (`docsStuffLimitChars`, Noteling's name for it), up from 120,000. With 305,000 characters, sending them whole took 1.9 to 2.2 seconds on `claude-sonnet-5-5` against 2.8 to 6.4 reading them through tools, and it was faster on every model tried.
- 2026-11-12: The brain's default model is now `claude-sonnet-5-5` at low effort. `claude-opus-5-5` took 1.7 seconds an answer last night and 2.8 to 4.8 this morning, with one lookup at 9.4 in a session; Sonnet took 1.6 to 1.8 and answered the same questions correctly, "not in the notes" included. `claude-haiku-5-5` was faster still (1.3 to 1.5) and got one answer of three wrong on the larger notes.
- 2026-11-12: When the notes are too big to send whole, the Claude Code CLI reads a copy that holds the notes and nothing else. A folder a person adds may have other files in it, and Stand should only ever say what is in a note.
- 2026-11-12: For a test run, `STAND_BRAIN` names another settings file, and `STAND_CONTEXT` makes one folder the only notes, ignoring `sources`.
- 2026-11-12: The brief is one note, `brief.md`, edited in the panel and kept until changed. It goes into the instructions after the talk, and where the two differ the brief wins. There is no list of meetings yet: the app knows a talk and a brief.
- 2026-11-12: Told by the brief to leave the name's origin out of the talk, Stand left it out, and then told someone who asked that it did not know where the name comes from. The brief's framing now says it changes the talk and never what Stand knows. Three wordings: it answered in 1 run of 2, then 2 of 3, then 3 of 3.
- 2026-11-12: A rehearsal has no brief unless `--brief` gives one. Otherwise whatever was left in `brief.md` would change every test.
- 2026-11-12: A note passed during the meeting is one text field and one key. Stand decides from the note's words whether to say something now or only to know it. Asking the person to choose would be one more thing to get wrong mid-meeting.
- 2026-11-12: Text sent to Gemini while Stand is speaking stops it mid-word, as a voice does. In the first run two notes each cut Stand off a word or two into a sentence, and it started the sentence again. So a note now waits: Stand gets it at once when silent, and in its next pause when talking.
- 2026-11-12: The pause is found in Stand's own audio (at least 0.34 seconds below a level that a breath stays under), and the session makes the cut itself there. Timing it by the model's transcript did not work: the fragments' timing ran up to 0.9 seconds ahead of the sound and 2.5 behind within one turn, and one cut landed in the middle of the word "transcript".
- 2026-11-12: The words Stand is told the audience heard, after such a cut, end at the punctuation mark nearest in time to the pause, a sentence end being preferred. After a whole sentence Stand is told to start the next one; after a comma, to carry on mid-sentence. If no long pause comes within 6 seconds a short one will do, and after 9 seconds the note reaches it wherever it is.
- 2026-11-12: Stand has a third tool, `nothing_to_say`. A voice model answers every text message out loud: handed a note that only told it something, it said "silent" in one run and "I understand, I am ready for any other questions" in the next. The tool is its way to answer without speaking.
- 2026-11-12: A reply to a note that lasts under a second is not played. It is the same stray word again, and the tool does not always prevent it.
- 2026-11-12: With the tool to hand, Stand once read a note that asked it to tell the audience something and chose silence; the message also said never to read the note out. The message now sorts notes into two kinds and says what to do with each. Since then: 21 of 21 said, 17 of 17 kept quiet.
- 2026-11-12: A note that arrives while someone is speaking to Stand is given to it at once, as a message that needs no reply, so that it can use the note in its answer. That still makes it start answering early about one time in three, so whatever it says before that person has finished is not played. Telling it to call `nothing_to_say` and wait made it skip the answer altogether once in three, so the message only says to let the person finish.
- 2026-11-12: After a note stops Stand mid-talk, the stage manager says "carry on" after 1.2 seconds, not 2.5, and at once if Stand calls `nothing_to_say`. Before this it stalled for 3.2 to 5.8 seconds after four of five such notes.
- 2026-11-12: When Stand is cut off, only the fragments heard in full are quoted back to it. A fragment's time is when its sound starts, so the old rule counted the one being spoken as heard, and Stand could skip its last words. Now it may repeat a few instead.
- 2026-11-12: The panel is left out of screen sharing unless the Stand menu says otherwise, because it now holds the brief and the notes. Control-Option-N puts the keyboard in the note field; Return passes the note, Escape gives up, and both give the keyboard back to the app that had it.
- 2026-11-12: The test notes' disclaimer ("nothing in this file is true") moved to their folder's README, which is not read. Sonnet declined to answer from a file that says that about itself.
- 2026-11-12: The lookup script's mid-talk line is now one sentence ("Sorry to interrupt, but how many…"). With a full stop after "Sorry to interrupt", Stand answered "no problem, go ahead" over the rest of the question in two runs of two and lost it. That is the known gap of jumping in at a pause, and it was hiding what the script is there to test.
- 2026-11-12: **For this phase the brain may be an external tool on the computer, run from its command line, and Claude Code is fine. This is a compromise, not the design**: those are the user's words. It loosens the earlier rule that the brain must not depend on Claude Code. It settles how the brain is reached for now, not what the brain should be.
- 2026-11-12: Under that compromise, the brain is one Claude Code process for the whole meeting, kept open and asked one thing after another, not a process for each question. Kept open it answered from small notes in 0.8 to 0.9 seconds against 1.4 to 2.1, and it remembers the questions before.
- 2026-11-12: What the brain may do is set by Stand, not by what Claude Code is allowed on this Mac. It reads the notes and, with `web` on, searches the web. For anything else (one particular web page, a file outside the notes, a tool from an MCP server) it asks Stand's human first. It has no tool that runs a command or changes a file.
- 2026-11-12: The brain's file tools work in a copy that holds the notes and nothing else, and `Read` is not on its list of tools allowed without asking. On that list it could read any file on the Mac, and anyone in a meeting could ask Stand what a file says.
- 2026-11-12: The brain's question to its human is a line in the panel with Allow and No, open for 20 seconds. No answer is a no. Refused, the brain is told to say only that the thing cannot be checked just now, so that Stand does not announce to the meeting that it was refused.
- 2026-11-12: MCP servers reach the brain in two ways: a file named by `mcpConfig`, and `claudeCodeSetup`, which brings this Mac's own Claude Code setup. The second is off unless turned on: the user's own connectors should not be within reach of a meeting until they decide so.
- 2026-11-12: If the brain has not answered in 5 seconds, Stand is told so, says it is taking a moment, and carries on. The answer reaches it later the way a note from its human does: at once if it is silent, in its next pause if it is talking, never across someone who is speaking. After 60 seconds the brain is stopped and Stand says it could not find it. Before today a lookup was dropped after 15 seconds.
- 2026-11-12: With a brain that can reach beyond the notes, Stand has the lookup tool even when there are no notes. That replaces the rule of 2026-11-11 that it gets the tool only when the notes folder has files.
- 2026-11-12: The brain never searches the web about Stand, the people in the meeting or their own projects. The web knows nothing true about them, and other things are called Stand.
- 2026-11-12: The brain is started, and asked one thing, before the voice model is told it has a brain. If that fails or takes over 12 seconds, Stand goes without the lookup tool for that meeting: "I don't know" is better than "let me check" and then nothing.
- 2026-11-12: During the talk Stand does not look up something off its subject, such as the weather; in the questions at the end it may. In the north-star run it said "I can look that up, but let's focus on the presentation for now", and went back to the talk.
- 2026-11-12: The lookup section of Stand's instructions went back to its concrete form ("You have notes…"). Rewritten to speak of "your brain: a helper", it had Stand look up the first question after the talk in only 3 runs of 7; back in the old form, 3 of 3. A line in its identity saying its human must say yes before "such a tool" is used was there for some of those runs and was taken out as well, untested by itself.
- 2026-11-12: Claude Code's skills are not given to the brain. Most need a tool that runs commands, which the brain does not have.
- 2026-11-12: A run's folder name is settled when the session opens, with a suffix if another session took the name in the same second. Two rehearsals started together had written into one folder.
- 2026-11-12: **The app asks the person as little as possible.** The user's rule for this phase, in their words: "useful but creepy" before "useless and annoying", and no asking for permission or confirmation every time. It applies to the whole app, not only to the brain.
- 2026-11-12: Under that rule, this Mac's own Claude Code setup comes into the brain unless turned off (`claudeCodeSetup`), and the brain reads files, reads web pages and uses the tools of its MCP servers without asking. The Allow and No line from earlier the same day stays for one case: a tool whose name says it cannot be undone (send, reply, forward, delete, remove, trash, cancel, pay, purchase, buy, publish, post, transfer; `prompts/ask-before.txt`). A person asked by a third party to send something from someone else's account would glance at that someone first. `"ask": "never"` removes it.
- 2026-11-12: Two things the brain refuses without asking, because asking would be the annoying way and allowing the costly one: a file that looks like it holds secrets, and searching inside files outside the notes. A plain search of a folder read a hidden `.env` and gave up the key in it (a decoy). A folder that should be searchable is added as a source of notes, where only notes are read.
- 2026-11-12: Nobody is asked whether a GitHub repository is private. If GitHub will not show it without a token, the GitHub CLI's login on this Mac is tried, and remembered for the next check. The "It is private" tick in the panel is gone.
- 2026-11-12: A rehearsal keeps this Mac's own Claude Code setup out of the brain unless `--own-setup` is given. Its questions are scripted, and should not go looking in the person's own mail and calendar.
- 2026-11-12: Stand's voice still only passes the brain things to find out. Someone who asks Stand to do something (send, cancel) gets it done only if the voice model treats it as a lookup. Making "do this" a thing of its own is not built.
- 2026-11-12: Stand can be packaged for another Mac (`mac/scripts/package.sh`). The packaged app carries who Stand is and how it behaves; the person's own files live in `~/Stand`, made on first use. A file there at the same path wins over the app's, so a new build brings new prompts and a prompt can still be changed on that Mac.
- 2026-11-12: On a Mac with no key for the voice model, the panel asks for one, once, and keeps it in `~/Stand/.env`. It is the one question the app cannot go without.
- 2026-11-12: On a Mac without Claude Code, Stand runs with no brain and the panel says so. It used to refuse to start a session.
- 2026-11-12: The package is built for Apple silicon and Intel, signed with the Developer ID and the hardened runtime. It is not sent to Apple for notarizing unless the user says so: that goes out under their developer account.
- 2026-11-12: A packaged app shows its build date in the panel, so that a report from another Mac says which build it was.
- 2026-11-12: Stand notarizes with a saved Apple login of its own (`stand-notary`) and no longer falls back to the one Noteling's release script uses. The user wanted Stand independent of Noteling here. The Developer ID certificate stays shared, because Apple ties it to the developer account and not to an app. A different developer name would take a company account, which is not for now.
- 2026-11-12: Stand can sit in a discussion without giving a talk (the panel's Talk choice "None: join the discussion"). It is another role (`prompts/attendee.md`) and another character (`identity/character-attendee.md`) for the same Stand. It speaks when it is spoken to, when someone asks the whole room for a fact it can find, when the discussion reaches what its brief says it is there for, and when its human passes it a note saying so. Otherwise it says nothing.
- 2026-11-12: In a discussion the stage manager never says "carry on", and Stand has no floor to open. Silence is the usual state there, not a stall.
- 2026-11-12: Saying nothing in a discussion is the same tool call as after a note (`nothing_to_say`). The voice service has a way of its own for a model to let speech pass ("proactive audio"). It was tried and accepted by the service, and Stand behaved no differently with it, so it is not used.
- 2026-11-12: Stand's own software now marks when someone starts and stops speaking, and the voice service no longer decides that for itself. Left to itself the service lost the start of whatever was said about a second after someone else had finished, which is when it is taking its own turn: Stand's name at the head of a question came through as "And" or not at all, and in the north-star script the off-topic question and the goodbye lost their first words in 3 runs of 3. Marked by Stand, they were whole in 3 of 3. Tried first, without effect: giving the model the silence between utterances, a shorter start-of-speech delay (worse: more misheard scraps), a longer end-of-speech silence (it moves the gap that is lost), taking the "say nothing" tool away, and not acknowledging its calls.
- 2026-11-12: How Stand marks speech (`SpeechMarker`): sound is judged in frames of 20 ms. A frame is loud when it is over 500 out of 32768 and over three times the quietest moment of the last four seconds, so a steady noise raises the bar and a voice does not. An utterance starts at four loud frames among the last ten, which a click or a key press does not reach. It is handed over from 0.3 seconds before that and ends after 0.7 seconds of quiet. While Stand itself is being heard, cutting in takes seven loud frames of ten, two of them at one and a half times the bar. `STAND_OWN_ACTIVITY=off` goes back to the service's detection, and `STAND_VOICE_LEVEL` sets the 500.
- 2026-11-12: While nobody speaks Stand sends the service nothing, and after about two minutes of nothing the service closes the session as abandoned. A tenth of a second of silence every 20 seconds keeps it open.
- 2026-11-12: Asked "anything else from you before we finish?", Stand answered by calling `nothing_to_say`, in 33 runs of 37: the tool's name is the answer it had in mind. The role and the tool's description now say that a question put to Stand always gets a spoken answer, even "no, nothing more from me".
- 2026-11-12: In a discussion the first 0.9 seconds of anything Stand says are held back, and a remark that ends inside them is played only if it has two words or more. A single word, or a sound with no words, is a slip and is dropped. The first form of this dropped everything under 0.9 seconds, and swallowed "Let me check that" and "I don't know".
- 2026-11-12: In a note from its human, "I" is the human. Stand had passed on "Tell them I will bring the name badges" as "I will bring the name badges". The note's framing now says so.
- 2026-11-12: With nothing to go on, Stand has no view and says so. Asked "what do you think of that?" in a meeting it had no brief for, it once said the date "works well for me" and once went to look up a calendar.

# Stand

An AI meeting attendant that speaks up by voice. Read `identity/stand.md` first: it is the project's identity and also the text Stand knows itself by, so keep it true.

## Rules that shape the code

- Voice in, voice out. Never route the live path through a transcript (speech-to-text, then a text model, then text-to-speech). A text model may sit behind the voice model and hand it the point to make, never a script.
- Behaviour lives in plain text: `prompts/`, `identity/`, `talks/`. Change those before changing code.
- Build a rough version and look at what it does. Record each on-the-spot choice as one line in `DECISIONS.md`.
- **Ask the person as little as possible.** The user's rule, 2026-11-12: in this phase Stand would rather be "useful but creepy" than "useless and annoying". (One thing starts out otherwise since Stand became something other people download: what the brain may reach beyond the notes. See "The brain, learned from runs".) No permission or confirmation prompt as a matter of course, anywhere in the app: do the thing, and show that it was done (the panel's status line, the run's transcript). Before adding a question, a checkbox or an "are you sure", look for a way to go without it. What still asks, and what is refused without asking, is listed under the brain below; add to either list only for a reason a person in the meeting would give.
- The north star is the self-introduction talk (`talks/introducing-stand.md`). `poc/heckler/north-star.json` is its test.
- The short-term goal, the user's on 2026-11-13, is what they called "an advanced mutation of our north star": in a real meeting, Stand introduces one of their company's internal systems from a talk its brain prepared out of that system's documents and code, and answers questions by looking them up. `poc/heckler/system-talk.json` is its test, with Stand's own repository as the system. Judge work by whether it brings that meeting closer.
- The second scenario is a discussion among people that Stand sits in without giving a talk. `prompts/attendee.md` is its role, and `poc/heckler/discussion.json` and the other `discussion-*.json` scripts are its tests.

## The repository

- Stand is open source since 2026-11-14: `github.com/noteling/stand`, public, under Apache 2.0 (`LICENSE`, and `NOTICE` in the form Noteling's has). The user's decision, after asking what staying closed was worth. The answer was little: the idea is no secret, the code is cheap, and the packaged app already carries every prompt as plain text.
- **Everything committed is published.** Never commit a recording or anything else from `runs/`, a key, the person's brief, settings or notes, a talk prepared for a meeting, or the name of a system or a person at the user's company. Read `git status` before `git add`.
- Commit and push when the user says to, not before. Changes go on a branch and reach `main` through a pull request, as they do in Noteling.
- A release is a GitHub release tagged `v<version>` with `Stand-<version>.dmg` and `SHA256SUMS.txt`, named as Noteling names its own. It is put up as a draft first: nobody but a person can check that the downloaded app hears and speaks, and the user publishes, or says to, after that.
- What keeps the person's own files out: the top-level `.gitignore` (`/brief.md`, `/brain.json`, `/context/*`) and `talks/.gitignore` (everything but Stand's own two talks). A new talk that should ship with Stand has to be named in the second.
- `brain.json` is not in the repository. Without one the settings are the defaults in `BrainSettings`. Keep those equal to `mac/Resources/brain.default.json`, which is what a packaged app starts with.
- The top-level `.gitignore` is also what the brain is kept from when this repository is its source (`poc/heckler/system-brain.json`): a line added there changes what that test's brain can read. `Ignore` follows a `!` line when folders are walked, so the two talks and `context/README.md` are still notes there; the rules handed to the brain's own search cannot say "except".
- `talk-room.json` and `hearing-probe.json` need `runs/2026-11-13T16-20-06-gemini-app`, which has people's voices in it and is not published. They run only on the Mac that has it.

## Layout

- `identity/`, `talks/`, `prompts/`: what Stand knows, the talk it gives, and how it behaves. These carry over to the product.
  - Giving a talk: `prompts/presenter.md`, `identity/character.md`, the talk, `prompts/voice-brief.md`, `prompts/provider-gemini.md`, `prompts/voice-look-it-up.md`. The stage manager hands the talk over a section at a time: `prompts/stage-manager-begin-section.txt`, `stage-manager-section.txt`, `-section-after-cut.txt` (points of the last section that were not heard, then the next), `-section-resume.txt` (back to a section Stand was stopped in), `-section-last.txt`, `-sections-done.txt`.
  - Preparing a talk before the meeting: `prompts/brain-prepare.md` (what the brain is told, and the form of the plan), `prompts/brain-prepare-request.txt`. The plan it writes goes under `talks/`.
  - A talk given with its pages on a screen: `prompts/voice-screen.md` (what Stand is told about the screen), `prompts/stage-manager-screen-shown.txt` and `stage-manager-screen-failed.txt` (the line that goes in front of a section, saying what the audience can now see, or that the page did not come up), `prompts/tool-show-page.txt` and the `prompts/screen-page-*.txt` files (a page that someone in the audience asked for). `prompts/brain-prepare.md` says how the brain names a page for a section.
  - The brain's watch over a talk: `prompts/brain-watch.md` (its rules, with the plan of the talk at the end), `prompts/brain-watch-request.txt` (what it is shown each time it is asked), `prompts/stage-manager-section-moved.txt` (how a section the talk was moved to is handed to Stand) and `prompts/stage-manager-wait.txt` (what Stand is told when it was asked to wait and went on talking). `prompts/presenter.md` says, under "When you are stopped", what Stand does when someone asks it to wait.
  - The ear for short sounds in a talk: `prompts/ear.md` (the listening model's rules: what is someone calling for the speaker, and what is not) and `prompts/ear-request.txt` (the line that goes with each sound). `prompts/stage-manager-called.txt`, `-called-unknown.txt` and `-called-heard.txt` are what Stand is told at the cut when the ear has had it stopped: that someone spoke to it in a word or two, with the last words heard, with "what you had just started" when there are none to quote, and with neither when nothing was lost. `prompts/presenter.md` says, under "When you are stopped", what Stand does when someone calls for it and has not yet said what they want.
  - Whose voice Stand speaks with: `prompts/voices-gemini.txt` (the thirty voices the live model takes by name, which is the panel's Voice menu) and `prompts/voice-sample.md` (the lines a sample says).
  - Sitting in a discussion (no talk; `Settings.talk` is empty): `prompts/attendee.md`, `identity/character-attendee.md`, and the `prompts/attend-*` files, which are the discussion's versions of the brief's framing, the lookup section, the "say nothing" tool and the cut-off messages. `Project.instructions` puts either set together.
  - The prompter. In a discussion: `prompts/brain-prompter.md` (its rules), `prompts/brain-prompter-request.txt` (what it is asked each time), `prompts/attend-sheet.txt` (how its sheet reaches Stand), `prompts/note-from-prompter.txt` and `prompts/stage-manager-cue.txt` (how a point worth saying reaches Stand). In a talk, where it is Stand's memory and prompts nothing: `prompts/brain-prompter-talk.md`, the same request file, and `prompts/talk-sheet.txt`.
- `mac/`: the native Mac app, a Swift package with two targets.
  - `StandCore`: `GeminiLive` (the WebSocket client; wire format captured from the JavaScript SDK), `AudioIO` (microphone with voice processing, plus playback), `SystemAudioTap` (the call's audio, through a Core Audio process tap), `StandSession` (the stage-manager logic, ported from `poc/src/session.js` and since extended), `SpeechMarker` (decides from the room's sound when someone starts and stops speaking, and in a talk whether a sound made while Stand speaks has gone on long enough to count; it offers a sound that has not, as soon as the sound goes quiet, for a closer listen, and can be told that the sound was someone speaking after all; no state but what it is fed, so a recording can be played through it), `Ear` (the closer listen: the Mac's own sound classifier says whether a short sound was words or a noise, and a small listening model, asked over HTTPS one sound at a time, says whether the words were someone calling for the speaker; no session and no process of its own), `Spend` (what a session cost, rebuilt turn by turn, and what the kept sessions add up to), `Prompter` (the sheet the prompter keeps in front of Stand in a discussion: the form of its reply and of what it is asked), `Preparation` (has the brain write a talk for a meeting, and `TalkPlan`, which cuts a plan into its numbered sections), `Screen` (what a talk's pages are shown on: one call that shows a page and answers with what came up; with it `TalkPlan.Page`, which is a plan's `Show:` line, and `Pages`, which finds a named page inside the folders Stand was given and nowhere else, and says which of a plan's pages are not there, `Pages.missing`, without putting anything up), `Watch` (the brain keeping watch over a talk: what it is shown, `WatchState`, and the one line it may answer, `WatchCommand`; no logic of its own, so the session and `--probe watch` share it), `Voices` (the voices Stand can speak with, read from `prompts/voices-gemini.txt`; refuses a name that is not on the list; fetches a sample of one in a short session of its own), `SpeechPlayer` (plays a sample through the speakers as it arrives: no microphone, no voice processing), `Project` (reads the text files).
  - The brain, also in `StandCore`: `Brain` (the protocol and `brain.json`), `Notes` (the sources, each a folder: the documents in them are read as notes, and everything else is counted, so that the brain can be told where it is; re-read when a file changes), `PageCopies` (a page from its human's browser as a source: where its copy is kept under `linked/pages/`, what the note says, and what a pasted address becomes in `brain.json`; the reading itself is the app's, `PageReading`), `GitHubSource` (a repository's notes kept as a copy in `linked/`, fetched Noteling's way: GitHub's API, no git), `ClaudeCodeBrain` (the brain for this phase: the local `claude` CLI, kept open for the whole meeting as one process and asked one thing after another; it reads the notes, may search the web, and asks Stand's human before anything else; a second such process is the prompter, `BrainFactory.makePrompter`, which has the notes and nothing else, and in a talk the plan of the talk as well; a third, `makePreparer`, writes a talk before a meeting, with a stronger model and fifteen minutes; a fourth, `makeWatch`, keeps watch over a talk, with the plan of the talk and nothing else, so that its one line comes back in under a second), `MessagesBrain` (the Messages API over raw HTTPS, direct or through a gateway: notes only, never run).
  - `Stand`: the app (a floating panel, `SessionController`, the hush and note hotkeys) and its command-line modes `--probe`, `--rehearse`, `--render-panel`. `PageWindow` is the screen's hand and eye for one page at a time: it brings a heading to the top, follows a section down the page, scrolls when asked, and looks at what is there by asking the page or by reading a picture of it (`PictureEye`, the Mac's own text recognition). What the page is put up on is a `PageSurface`, which only loads a page and runs a script in it: `WebViewSurface` is a window of Stand's own, which draws a document from its Markdown (`MarkdownPage`) or loads an address; `BrowserSurface` is a window Stand opens in the person's own Chrome, driven with Apple Events built by hand (`BrowserEvents`). `PageReading` reads the pages among the sources through that browser and keeps their copies. `Listener` plays a rehearsal's two sides through the speakers (`--hear`). `FaultyScreen` fails on purpose, for rehearsals. `WatchReplay` plays a finished talk to the brain's watch (`--probe watch`). `EarProbe` plays a recording of a room, or a list of sounds, to the ear (`--probe ear`). `Doctor` says what this Mac gives Stand to work with, and `ProblemReport` zips that with the last session for sending.
- The three ways anything but sound reaches Stand, each with its own files:
  - The brief: `brief.md` (the panel edits it), framed by `prompts/voice-brief.md`, part of the instructions.
  - Notes to look things up in: `context/`, plus the folders and GitHub repositories under `sources` in `brain.json`. `linked/` holds the repositories' copies; never edit it, every update replaces it.
  - A note passed during the meeting: `StandSession.tell`, with `prompts/stage-manager-note*.txt`.
- Stand asking its human: the tool `ask_my_human` (`prompts/tool-ask-my-human.txt`, `prompts/voice-ask-your-human.md`), `StandSession.answer(question:with:)`, and the `prompts/human-*.txt` messages. The answer travels the note lane.
- `brain.json`: how the brain reaches its model, where the notes are, and what the brain may reach beyond them (`web`, `mcpConfig`, `claudeCodeSetup`, `allowWithoutAsking`).
- **The external CLI as the brain is a compromise for this phase, not the design.** The user said so in those words on 2026-11-12. Do not build on it as if it were settled, and do not describe it as the design in anything Stand reads or says.
- `mac/scripts/package.sh`: packages the app for another Mac as `mac/dist/Stand-<date>.dmg`. `--release` makes a version to publish: notarized, only from a checkout with nothing uncommitted, as `mac/dist/Stand-<version>.dmg` with `SHA256SUMS.txt`; the version is `CFBundleShortVersionString` in `mac/Resources/Info.plist`. A packaged app carries `identity/`, `talks/` and `prompts/` inside it (`Project.defaults`) and keeps the person's own files in `~/Stand` (`Project.home`, or `STAND_HOME`). A file in that folder wins over the one inside the app (`Project.location`). In the repo nothing changes: the repo folder is the project folder.
- `docs/own-voice.md`: a voice of the person's own. What the voice service allows, why voice copying is held back, and the user's idea of converting Stand's voice on this Mac after the model. Researched on 2026-11-13 and not built: "not worth it yet". Read it before touching the subject again.
- `docs/noteling.md`: how Stand and Noteling (the user's other Mac app, at `../noteling`) are meant to work together, and what is proposed for Noteling. Do not edit the Noteling repo from here.
- `poc/`: the JavaScript rig the behaviour was worked out in, kept as it was when `StandSession` was ported. Lookups and the cut-off-ending rule exist only in Swift. It also holds the scripted audience's scripts (`poc/heckler/`), which the Mac app's `--rehearse` reads.
  - `src/session.js`: one Stand session; shared by the browser server and the scripted audience.
  - `src/adapters/`: one file per voice model, all with the same small interface (see `adapters/index.js`).
  - `src/heckler.js`: the scripted audience and its report.
  - `public/`: the page, which is only a microphone and a speaker.

## Commands (run from `poc/`)

- `npm start`: the page at http://localhost:5177
- `npm run heckler -- --provider mock|gemini|openai`: scripted audience; `mock` needs no key
- `npm run smoke`: the page in headless Chrome against the mock

## Commands for the Mac app (run from the repo root)

- `mac/scripts/build.sh [debug|release]`: builds and signs `mac/build/Stand.app`. `swift build --package-path mac` for a quick compile check.
- `mac/build/Stand.app/Contents/MacOS/Stand --rehearse [script.json] [--talk file.md]`: the scripted audience against the native session. No audio hardware, no permissions. Use this after any change to `StandSession`.
- `... --probe connect | selfhear | otherapp | tap [--raw] [--no-system-audio]`: one audio piece at a time. All but `connect` make sound, use the microphone, and need the app's macOS permissions (granted on this Mac on 2026-11-11). `tap` needs no voice model: it plays a recording from another program and reports how loud it arrived.
- `... --rehearse [script.json] --out-loud`: the same scripted audience through the real speakers, microphone and system audio tap, wired as the app wires them. About three minutes of sound for the north-star script. Use this after any change to `AudioIO` or `SystemAudioTap`.
- `... --probe brain "question" ["another" …] [--allow]`: questions to one kept-open brain, each timed. No voice model, no audio. `--allow` answers yes when the brain asks its human for something; without it the answer is no. Add `STAND_CONTEXT=poc/heckler/context` to use the test notes, or `STAND_BRAIN=poc/heckler/operator-brain.json` for the test notes plus a test MCP server (`poc/heckler/mcp/`).
- `STAND_BRAIN=poc/heckler/operator-brain.json ... --rehearse poc/heckler/lookup-operator.json --talk poc/heckler/short-talk.md`: the brain as an operator: a fact from the notes, a tool from the test MCP server (used without asking), and a web search slow enough for Stand to say so and come back to it. Use it after any change to `ClaudeCodeBrain`, the lookup prompts, or the session's handling of slow and late answers.
- `STAND_BRAIN=poc/heckler/operator-brain-ask.json ... --rehearse poc/heckler/lookup-operator-no.json --talk poc/heckler/short-talk.md`: the same brain set to ask before everything, with nobody saying yes: the Allow/No path, and what Stand says when refused.
- `STAND_BRAIN=poc/heckler/operator-brain.json ... --probe brain "Where has the Juniper order got to?" "Cancel the Harbour order." [--allow]`: looking uses a tool without asking; cancelling asks first.
- `... --probe sources [--add <folder or repository address>] [--remove N] [--list]`: the sources, with linked repositories checked: each one's notes, how many other files the brain can search there, and where they are as the brain is told. No voice model, no brain.
- `... --probe voice [name …] [--all] [--out folder] [--play] [--silently]`: a sample of each named voice (Kore when none is named, all thirty with `--all`), fetched as the panel's **Hear it** fetches it and kept as a WAV file. No microphone, and no sound unless `--play`. `--silently` puts the sample through the panel's own player at no volume and says how much of it came out. `--probe voice --as-the-panel` presses **Hear it** on the controller the panel uses, at no volume: it chooses a voice, hears it, chooses another and the first again while its sample is still arriving, and hears it a third time from what is kept; it prints PASS or what went wrong. About a quarter of a cent a voice. Use both after any change to `Voices`, `SpeechPlayer`, `SessionController.hearVoice` or the setup message in `GeminiLive.connect`.
- `STAND_BRAIN=poc/heckler/screen/brain.json ... --probe screen poc/heckler/screen/talk.md [--eye picture] [--show] [--out folder] [--places]`: the hand and the eye with no voice. Says first which of the talk's pages are not there (`Pages.missing`), then puts up each page the talk names and the first again, and says what came up and how long it took; pictures go into a folder. `--places` prints, for each section longer than the window, the place on the page found for each of its points: read it after any change to the matching in `PageWindow.follow`. No credit. A talk outside the project is given by its full path. Use it after any change to `PageWindow`, `Pages` or the `Show:` line. `--probe screen <talk> --as-the-panel` goes the app's own way with nobody at it (the controller the panel uses opens the window for a talk with pages, puts the first page up, and opens none for a talk without) and prints PASS or what went wrong: use it after any change to `SessionController.openScreen`, `showScreenNow` or `checkTalkForPages`.
- `... --probe screen <talk> --in-chrome` (or `STAND_SCREEN=chrome` in front of any run, a rehearsal with `--screen` and `--as-the-panel` included): the same check with the pages in a window Stand opens in the person's own Chrome. No credit. It opens a window in their browser, and closes its tab before it ends. Two things have to be allowed there first, and the output says which is missing; `--probe doctor` says where both stand without opening anything. `STAND_BROWSER=<bundle id>` names another browser built on Chrome. `STAND_BROWSER_PID=<pid>` sends everything to one process, which is how a second copy of Chrome with a scratch profile is used for tests, with nothing of the person's own profile touched: write `{"browser":{"allow_javascript_apple_events":true}}` to `<a scratch folder>/Default/Preferences`, start it with `open -n -g -a "Google Chrome" --args --user-data-dir=<that folder> --no-first-run`, and find its process with `pgrep -f`. Quit that copy afterwards. Use it, in such a copy, after any change to `BrowserSurface` or `BrowserEvents`.
- `python3 -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1 --directory poc/heckler/screen/site`, and with that running `STAND_BRAIN=poc/heckler/screen/brain.json ... --probe screen poc/heckler/screen/site-talk.md --places [--in-chrome]`: the screen on pages Stand did not draw. A wiki-like page (a bar fixed over the top and a second that sticks under it, a menu with headings of its own, one of them named like a section, words that arrive 0.7 seconds after the page, a last section too near the end to reach the top) and an app-like page (a panel that scrolls inside a window that does not, a toolbar stuck to the top of the panel, headings that are not `h2`). No credit. Use it after any change to the scripts in `PageWindow` (`tools`, `scroll(to:)`, `follow`, `bring`, `scroll`, `atTheTop`), in both kinds of window, together with `talk.md`, `long-talk.md` and a talk the brain prepared: the output for those three must not change by a line, so keep a copy from before the change and compare. Look at the pictures the own window leaves, too: the page's own answer was once right while the heading sat behind a bar.
- `... --probe page <address> [--all]`: what Stand reads of a page, printed and not kept: its title, its headings and its own words as a note would have them. Read in the person's Chrome; `STAND_SCREEN=window` in front reads in Stand's own window, which gets what anyone can see and needs nothing allowed. No credit. Use it after any change to `PageWindow.read`, on the two test pages and on a real one or two.
- `... --probe sources --add-page <address>` adds a page as a source and reads it; `--read-pages` reads every page source again; `--as-the-panel-adds <address>` goes the panel's own way with nobody at it (adds the page on the controller the panel uses, waits for it to be read, checks the list, removes it and its copy) and prints PASS or what went wrong. Run these with `STAND_BRAIN` naming a settings file outside the repository, or they write to the person's own `brain.json`; the copies land in `linked/pages/` whichever settings file is used, so remove the sources again. Use `--as-the-panel-adds` after any change to `SessionController.addPage`, `readPages`, `PageReading` or `PageCopies`.
- The whole of it with no voice, after any change to `prompts/brain-prepare.md`'s lines about pages, `PageCopies`, or `Pages.locate`: with the test pages served and a scratch settings file, `--add-page` both of them, `--probe prepare "Introduce Parcel Desk to people who have just joined the mailroom team. About two minutes. Show the pages as you go."`, read the plan (every `Show:` line an address with a heading the page has), and `--probe screen <the talk> --places` in both kinds of window.
- `STAND_BRAIN=poc/heckler/screen/brain.json ... --rehearse poc/heckler/screen.json --talk poc/heckler/screen/talk.md --screen`: the screen's test, two minutes, about 16 cents. A question about the page, "can you go back to the previous page", and a page asked for after the talk. `--screen-fails 3` makes the third page asked for fail; `--eye picture` looks by reading a picture of the window; `--show-screen` puts the window on the display. `... --rehearse poc/heckler/screen-scroll.json --talk poc/heckler/screen/long-talk.md --screen` is the scrolling test, a minute and a half and about 12 cents: a section longer than the window, a request to scroll back to the top in the middle of it, and one to scroll down after the talk. The report has a table, "The screen", and the run's folder has a picture for each time the screen was asked for a page. Use it, with and without `--screen-fails 3`, and with the north-star script, after any change to `moveScreen`, `showPage`, `sectionComing`, `nextInTalk`, the screen's prompts, or `prompts/voice-screen.md`. `--screen` and `--show-screen` run the binary with the app's run loop, which a window needs; every other rehearsal still parks the main thread. `... --rehearse poc/heckler/screen-any.json --talk <any talk with pages> --screen` is a scripted audience for a talk of the person's own: someone asks to go back a page after 70 seconds of Stand's speech, and for the first page at the end. With `--show-screen --hear [volume]` a person can watch it and hear both sides through the speakers, with no microphone (`--hear 0` plays at no volume and says how much went through; never use `--hear` above 0 when nobody is there to listen).
- `STAND_BRAIN=poc/heckler/screen/brain.json ... --rehearse poc/heckler/screen-go-on.json --talk poc/heckler/screen/talk.md --screen`: the talk steered in words. Someone asks for the page before and, when Stand has shown it, says "Okay, thank you. You can continue." About 13 cents. `... screen-steer.json ...`: someone asks Stand to stop for a moment and comes back ten seconds later, and someone asks to skip ahead to a later part. About 15 cents. The report has a table, "The watch, and the hand that follows the mouth". `--no-watch` on either (and `STAND_HAND_FOLLOWS=off` in front of the first) shows what happens without them, which is the fault. Use both after any change to `keepWatch`, `heed`, `movePlace`, `backWithTheVoice`, `stopToWait`, `prompts/brain-watch.md` or the hand-over (`nextInTalk`, `sectionComing`), together with the screen's own tests and the north-star script, and run each several times. In a script, a line with `afterQuietSeconds` is a follow-up: it is said once Stand has answered the line before it and then been quiet that long, into the silence and not over Stand.
- `... --probe watch <run folder> [--every 5] [--from seconds] [--to seconds] [--show-asks]`: plays a talk that has already happened to the brain's watch, and prints what it would have said each time it would have been asked, with how long each answer took. No voice model, no sound, no credit. What the run did stays as it was: the answers are printed, not acted on. A run made since 2026-11-15 is replayed exactly, because a talk's record now keeps every piece of Stand's speech with the moment it arrived and the moment its sound began; an older one is rebuilt from its transcript. Try a change to `prompts/brain-watch.md` here first: over a run that went right it should say NOTHING throughout, and over one that went wrong it should speak up at the moment it went wrong. `STAND_WATCH_MODEL=<model>` in front tries another model as the watch.
- `... --probe ear <run folder or room.wav> [--listen]`: plays a recording of a room through the marker as a talk has it, and for each short sound says what the ear makes of it: words or a noise by the Mac's own classifier, and with `--listen` a call for Stand or not by the listening model, with how long after the sound began Stand would have stopped. Nothing is acted on. No session and no sound; the classifier is free, and `--listen` costs about a hundredth of a cent a sound. `... --probe ear --sounds poc/heckler/ear/sounds.txt --listen [--times 2] [--all]` runs a list of 212 sounds that each say what they should be taken for: calls and things that are no call in three of the Mac's voices, and, on the Mac that has the recordings, a person's own tries and the laugh that has to pass. Use both after any change to `prompts/ear.md`, `Ear`, `earWordAtLeast`, the listening model (`STAND_EAR_MODEL=<model>` in front tries another) or what `SpeechMarker` offers (`offersAfter`, `call`). Over `runs/2026-11-15T18-43-13-gemini-app` the five tries that passed must each come out a call, and over `runs/2026-11-13T16-20-06-gemini-app` nothing may.
- `... --rehearse poc/heckler/talk-call.json`: the ear in a talk (the north-star talk; under three minutes, about 23 cents). Short sounds while Stand speaks, each saying what should become of it: a person's own "hello" and "excuse me" and a spoken "wait" should stop Stand, and "yeah", "right", a real laugh and "you go ahead" said to someone else should not. ## Things to know

- The Gemini adapter has run against the live service; the GPT-Live adapter has not. Both were written from vendor docs and SDK type definitions on 2026-11-11. Both APIs are new; check the installed SDK's types before changing them.
- How Gemini 3.8 Live behaves, learned from runs:
  - It speaks one turn and then waits, so `session.js` sends "carry on" after its turn ends.
  - It generates far ahead of what is heard. Transcript text and tool calls arrive when generated, so anything timed must use the session's virtual playhead (`playUntil`).
  - Any speech-like sound stops it, an "mm-hm" included. Its start-sensitivity and prefix-padding settings did not change that.
  - A message sent as a complete turn forces a spoken reply; realtime text may be ignored.
  - It counts everything it has sent as said, heard or not. So when it is cut off, the session tells it the last words that were heard (`tellWhereCut` in `StandSession`, `prompts/stage-manager-cut-off.txt`). Do not remove this: without it Stand skips whatever it had generated but not yet played, 7 to 10 seconds per interruption in the north-star run.
  - A short talk is generated whole in about five seconds, `open_the_floor` included. An interruption after that cancels the floor, and Stand does not call the tool again unless told to (`prompts/stage-manager-finish.txt`).
  - A "carry on" that reaches it when it has nothing to say can make it say a word such as "Silence" out loud. Keep the floor state right so that no such message is sent.
  - Its transcript of its own speech can be wrong: "..." for 21 seconds of real speech, "<!-- Silence -->" for a spoken word. Check loudness in `stand.wav`, or use `listen.js`.
  - A tool response with scheduling `WHEN_IDLE` is spoken about half a second after it is sent, when Stand is not talking. `SILENT` is not spoken.
  - Text sent to it while it is speaking stops it, exactly as a voice does: the server reports an interruption about 50 ms later, whether or not the turn had finished generating. So a note waits for a pause, the session makes the cut itself, and one message carries both the note and the last words heard (`landNotes`).
  - Text sent to it while it is silent always draws a turn in reply. Told to say nothing, it says a word such as "silent", or "I understand", out loud. It needs a way to answer without speech, which is the `nothing_to_say` tool. Do not remove the tool. A reply to a note shorter than a second is also not played (`shortestReplyToNote`).
  - With that tool to hand, a note that reaches it mid-speech can make it call the tool and stall. The mid-speech note prompts say to keep speaking, and the stage manager says "carry on" 1.2 seconds after a note stopped it, or at once when it calls the tool.
  - A message it need not answer (`inform`: `clientContent` with `turnComplete: false`) is taken in and remembered. Sent while someone is speaking, it still makes Stand start answering early about one time in three. What it starts to say before that person has finished is held back and dropped (`Held.whileListening`).
  - Its transcript of itself arrives in fragments of about a second of speech, ahead of the sound. A fragment's timing against the sound was off by as much as 0.9 seconds early and 2.5 late within one turn. Never time a cut by the transcript: find the pause in the audio (`inPause`), then take the wording from the nearest punctuation mark (`heardUpToMark`).
  - A brief that says to leave something out of the talk made it claim not to know that thing when asked. `prompts/voice-brief.md` now says the brief changes the talk, never what it knows.
  - Left to decide for itself when someone is speaking, the service loses the start of an utterance that begins about a second after the previous one ended, which is when the model is taking its own turn. A name at the head of a sentence goes ("Stand, do you know…" arrived as "And do you know…"), and a first word can come out as another language ("Thanks" as "再见"). So the session marks speech itself (`marksActivity`: `activityStart` and `activityEnd`, with 0.3 seconds of sound from before the utterance; `SpeechMarker` decides when). Do not go back to the service's detection without rerunning the north-star and discussion scripts both ways (`--service-activity`).
  - A real microphone hands the session 100 ms at a time; the scripted audience hands it 20. `SpeechMarker` cuts whatever it gets into 20 ms frames, so both are judged alike. Never write a rule there in terms of "a chunk".
  - The form of `SpeechMarker` now in the code (frames, the click filter, the cut-in rule) was first checked by replaying recordings, and then ran in sessions on the evening of 2026-11-12: `discussion.json` 11 of 11 in 5 runs, `discussion-pauses.json` and `discussion-pushback.json` 3 of 3 each, the north-star script 2 runs with every line heard whole. It has still not heard a real microphone live.
  - While an utterance is open the service does not let Stand speak, even when text is sent to it: in the north-star runs the cut-off message went out in the middle of each question and Stand answered a second after the question ended. Whether the same holds for a lookup's answer that comes back while someone is speaking has not been seen. If it does not hold, Stand would talk over them, and their carrying on would not stop it, because no new utterance starts.
  - With speech marked by the session, nothing is sent while the room is quiet, and after about two minutes of nothing the service closes the session ("client inactivity"). The session sends a tenth of a second of silence every 20 seconds.
  - The service's own way of letting a model not answer (`proactivity.proactiveAudio` in the setup) is accepted and changed nothing that was measured. Neither did `turnCoverage: TURN_INCLUDES_ALL_INPUT`. A `prefixPaddingMs` of 20 produced more misheard scraps, one of them "¿Qué?".
  - Asked "anything else from you?", it called `nothing_to_say` as its answer. A question put to it must be answered out loud; the role and the tool's description both say so. Keep both.
  - In a discussion it stays quiet well when told that most of the time the right thing to say is nothing. In 59 runs the guard against stray words caught one sound with no words to it, and swallowed "Let me check that" three times and "I don't know" once before it was narrowed to single words.
  - Stand cannot see its notes, and does not look up what it has no reason to think they hold: after a stretch of unrelated talk it answered "I don't know" to a question the notes answer, in 9 runs of 12. So the brain is asked before the meeting what the notes cover (`prompts/brain-outline.txt`), and its sentence or two goes into Stand's instructions (`prompts/notes-outline.md`). Keep that; it also stopped lookups of things Stand already knows.
  - Told that someone has started speaking while the model is still taking its turn on the utterance before, the service drops that turn, and with it the new utterance from its own transcript: 26 runs of 26. The model then catches what was said only some of the time. So in a discussion the start of such an utterance is kept back, with its sound, until the turn is over (`startHeld`, `turnFinishesWithin`), which is mostly a quarter of a second. Do not remove this: with it the transcript lost 0 lines of 466, and without it between a seventh and a third.
  - It hears one sentence as one utterance best. Several people's sentences in one utterance, with their pauses, cost it Stand's name at the head of the last one about half the time (21 answers in 43 runs against 22 in 22). Shortening the pauses inside the utterance did not help, nor did telling it that the last sentence matters most. `endsAfterWhenOut` was 1.5 seconds for one evening for this reason's opposite, to save turns; leave it equal to `ownActivityEndsAfter`.
  - Once in about sixty runs its speech had a glitch in the middle (a listener model called it laughter), its transcript showed markup there ("<action><primary_action>…"), and it then made the same point a second time.
  - Once in about nine hundred sessions (2026-11-16, a talk with pages and no brain) it wrote its tool calls into its own transcript instead of making them, from one moment to the end of the session: `<tools><show_page section="3"/></tools>`, and the same for `open_the_floor` and `nothing_to_say`. None of that was audible, and no call arrived: asked for a page, Stand said "Sure, one moment" and nothing came up. Nine more runs of the same kind did not show it, and no kept transcript before that day has it. Nothing catches it. If it comes again, the session could take such a line for the call it stands for.
- The brain, learned from runs:
  - Only the Claude Code CLI connection has run. `MessagesBrain` has never made a request: there is no Anthropic key or gateway on this machine.
  - `ClaudeCodeBrain` talks to the CLI in its stream-json form: one JSON object a line each way. A question goes in as `{"type":"user","message":{"role":"user","content":[{"type":"text","text":…}]}}`, and the answer is the `result` field of the line whose `type` is `result`.
  - With `--permission-prompt-tool stdio`, the CLI asks before a tool that needs permission: a line `{"type":"control_request","request_id":…,"request":{"subtype":"can_use_tool","tool_name":…,"input":{…}}}`. The reply is `{"type":"control_response","response":{"subtype":"success","request_id":…,"response":{"behavior":"allow","updatedInput":{…}}}}`, or `"behavior":"deny"` with a `message`. `--permission-prompts host` alone sends nothing. This was learned by trying it on CLI 2.1.322, not from documentation.
  - The CLI finds its login by the `USER` environment variable: without it, it answers "Not logged in". An app opened from Finder has `USER`; `ClaudeCodeBrain` sets it if it is missing.
  - What the brain may do is decided in `ClaudeCodeBrain`, where every permission question the CLI raises is answered (`can_use_tool`). There are two ways it runs, and which one is `ask` and `claudeCodeSetup` in `brain.json`:
    - **As a new copy of Stand starts, since 2026-11-14** (`"ask": "always"`, `"claudeCodeSetup": false`; the defaults in `BrainSettings` and in `mac/Resources/brain.default.json`, which must stay equal): the notes, the folders and a web search without asking; a question in the panel before anything else (a web page, a file outside the folders, an MCP tool); this Mac's Claude Code setup left out. The reason is that other people now get Stand, and whoever speaks in a meeting can ask it things: in `lookup-operator.json` a scripted question is answered through a connected tool with nobody asked.
    - **As the user runs it, "ask as little as possible"** (`"ask": "irreversible"`, `"claudeCodeSetup": true`, written out in this Mac's own `brain.json`, which is not in the repository): allowed without asking are other files on this Mac by full path, web pages, and every tool of the MCP servers it has, this Mac's own Claude Code setup included. Asked first, in the panel: only a tool whose name has a word from `prompts/ask-before.txt` (send, delete, cancel, pay…). `"ask": "never"` removes that too.
    - The test settings under `poc/heckler/` say `"ask": "irreversible"` in so many words, so the scripted tests run as they always have. A rehearsal on a Mac with no `brain.json` runs the cautious way: a web page or a file outside the folders would wait 20 seconds for a yes that nobody gives.
    - Refused without asking: a file that looks like it holds secrets (`looksSecret`), and `Grep` anywhere outside the folders it was given. The second is there because a plain search of a folder read a hidden `.env` and gave up the key in it (tried with a decoy on 2026-11-12). Do not loosen either: a key spoken into a meeting cannot be taken back.
    - Keep `Read` out of `--allowedTools`. On that list it never reaches `can_use_tool`, and the secrets check is skipped.
  - A rehearsal leaves this Mac's own Claude Code setup out (`STAND_CLAUDE_SETUP=off`, set by `--rehearse` unless `--own-setup` is given), so that scripted questions do not go looking in the person's own mail and calendar. `STAND_CONTEXT` leaves it out too. A `--probe brain` with the real `brain.json` does load it.
  - With no notes at all the brain still has to start (it may use the web): its program is started in an empty folder of its own (`NotesLibrary.standingPlace`), sources or no sources. That once failed, and only a probe with the real, empty settings showed it.
  - When GitHub will not show a repository without a token, Stand tries the GitHub CLI's login by itself and remembers that it was needed (`auth` in the copy's record). Nobody is asked whether a repository is private. That path has run once, against an address that does not exist; it has never fetched a real private repository. A `$NAME` token from `.env` has never been used.
  - The brain is got ready before the voice model is told it has one (`brainStartSeconds`). If it cannot start, Stand gets no lookup tool for that session.
  - How Stand is told about looking things up decides whether it does. In the afternoon of 2026-11-12 the lookup section was rewritten to speak of "your brain: a helper" where it had said "You have notes". Over the 7 runs with that wording, the first question after the talk was looked up in 3 and the order question in 2; with the concrete wording back, 3 of 3 each. For some of those 7 runs `identity/stand.md` also said its human must say yes before "such a tool" is used, which may have added to it and was not tested by itself. Keep `prompts/voice-look-it-up.md` concrete, and keep permission out of what Stand knows about itself: asking is the brain's business.
- The prompter, learned from runs on the night of 2026-11-12:
  - It is the user's idea, in their words: "why cant the brain be the teleprompter then?! … brain coordinate the mouth through some type of consistent adjustable on the fly generated teleprompter". The voice model hears and speaks; a second brain follows the transcript, knows the notes, and keeps a sheet in front of it: SO FAR, IN PLAY, WORTH SAYING, OWED. This is the only place a transcript is used. It decides what is put in front of Stand, never its words.
  - The sheet goes to Stand as a message it need not answer (`inform`), when IN PLAY or OWED has changed or a minute has passed, and not more often than every 20 seconds. WORTH SAYING goes the way a note does (`NoteFrom.prompter`), because acting on it takes a turn.
  - The prompter is asked when 160 characters have been said, and once in every silence of three seconds, whether or not anything new has been said: that is when it can tell that a question was left hanging. Asked at two and a half seconds, it judged the quiet too short to mean anything.

  - A point waits for a second and a half in which nobody is heard, Stand included. It is dropped if Stand speaks first (it may just have made the point by itself), if a note from the human or a promised answer is waiting, or after 30 seconds. Without the first it brought the overruled date back and Stand said it again. Without the second it guessed that "the Thursday" fell before a date in the brief.
  - A reply held back for a note or a collision must not outlive its turn (`held`, cleared at `turnComplete` when no sound came). Left in place, it took Stand's next remark for more of the same, swallowed it when someone spoke, and did not tell Stand, which then would not make the point again.
  - A line in `prompts/attendee.md` saying that the last sentence before a pause matters most went with 0 answers in 2 runs of the main script's closing question, against 35 of 38 without it. It is gone. Do not explain to the voice model how its sound is cut up.
  - The prompter can only use what the transcript has. That is why the two rules about utterances above matter to it as much as to Stand's own hearing.
  - A fact in the brief that asks for nothing was taken for a point to deliver. - A folder as the brain's boundary, learned on 2026-11-13:
  - It is the user's design: "it gives boundary, this is the folder you can use, and then the llm can just run grep". I had built a filtered copy first (file endings that count as code, limits, every file's text checked for keys) and they stopped it. Do not go back to copying, indexing or choosing files for the brain. When something in a folder gets in the way, use a rule against reading it.
  - The brain's program starts in an empty folder of its own and is given each source with `--add-dir`. Inside those folders Read, Grep and Glob never raise a permission question, so `can_use_tool` and `looksSecret` are not consulted there. What is kept from the brain inside its folders is kept by Claude Code's own rules against reading, written to a settings file for each start (`neverRead`, and the folder's top-level `.gitignore` as patterns). Those rules hold for Grep and Glob as well as Read: a planted key in a hidden `.env` was not found, read or listed.
  - The rules go by name. A key written inside an ordinary file of code or configuration is not caught; the brain's instructions say never to give one out, and asked outright for the project's key it refused. That is the whole of the protection there, and the user was told.
  - A plain folder that is not a git repository does not get its `.gitignore` honoured by the search. With Stand's own `runs/` (660 sessions) in reach, a question about the code took 7.9 seconds and six searches; with the `.gitignore` turned into rules, 4.2 and two. Keep that. (Stand's folder was a plain folder when this was measured, and has been a git repository since 2026-11-14.)
  - An answer that takes a search of the folder took 3 to 5 seconds on Stand's own repository, and the brain's activity is shown as "looking in <the source's name>".
  - Outside its folders nothing changed: it reads a file by full path, never one that looks like secrets, and a Grep there is refused.
  - A file that is not a note being added or changed does not restart the brain's program; a note changing does, as before.
  - A linked repository's copy holds documents and code, by ending, since this day (`Record.holds`); an older copy is fetched again once, which ran once (`lookup-sources.json`, the answer came from the copy).
  - `MessagesBrain` has the notes and nothing else: it has no search of its own to give a folder to. Never run.
- Rehearsals, learned the hard way:
  - Two sessions that open in the same second used to share one run folder and overwrite each other. The second now gets a suffix (`-b`).
  - The Mac's `say` reads a number such as 4417 slowly, with pauses, and Stand answers in the middle of it. Give things names in scripts ("the Juniper order").
  - A short question from a `say` voice is sometimes heard cut off, and Stand asks for it again or answers the fragment. In the afternoon of 2026-11-12 that happened in 4 of the first 9 runs. Run a script more than once before reading anything into one run. (Most of this was the service's speech detection losing first words; see above. With the session marking speech, the lookup script's questions were whole in 3 runs of 3.)
  - `say` obeys `[[slnc 1100]]` inside a line: a pause of that many milliseconds mid-sentence.
  - The transcript of the room is the service's own and is not what the model heard. Whole lines are missing from it in runs where Stand answered them correctly.
  - The words quoted back to Stand when it is cut off in a discussion must be its present remark's only (`holdOpening` clears the pieces). Quoted with the end of the previous remark, it took the message to be about the earlier point and made that again.
  - A scripted gap of 0.9 seconds hides whatever Stand says unasked: it begins, collides with the next speaker, is not played, and the line counts as quiet. To test that Stand keeps something to itself, leave four seconds after the line (`gapSeconds`), and read the "stray word" lines of the transcript as well as the score.
  - Stand beginning a remark at the same moment as someone else (`attend-not-heard.txt`) is now common in rehearsals: it gets a turn after every sentence, and the scripts leave exactly 0.9 seconds between people. It gives way, nothing of it is heard, and it makes the point when they have finished.
  - Paths in the day's diff that no run has ever taken: `hush` in a discussion, a connection replaced while someone is mid-sentence, a refused key, the shell being asked where `claude` is.
  - Do not run `mac/scripts/build.sh` while rehearsals are running from `mac/build/Stand.app`. `swift build --package-path mac` checks that the code compiles without touching the app, and `mac/.build/debug/Stand` runs the silent modes.
  - To judge how the speech detector would do on a real room without making a sound, run it offline over the `room.wav` of a session that used the real microphone (`runs/*-out-loud`, `runs/*-app`). On 2026-11-12 those showed a level of about 1 out of 32768 between voices and while Stand spoke, and voices in the thousands: the microphone's own echo cancelling leaves almost nothing.
- Packaging, learned on 2026-11-12:
  - To try the packaged app as another Mac would see it: run `mac/dist/Stand.app/Contents/MacOS/Stand` from outside the repo (`cd /`) with `STAND_HOME` set to an empty folder. From inside the repo it finds the repo and behaves as the repo's own build. A key can be given to such a test by linking the repo's `.env` into that folder.
  - A URL for a folder that does not exist yet is not known to be a folder, and a path worked out against it (`relativeTo:`) lands beside it, not inside. The first packaged run put `context` next to the home folder. `Project.root` now returns a URL that says it is a directory.
  - There is no Xcode on this Mac, only the command line tools, so `swift build --arch arm64 --arch x86_64` fails. The Intel half is built by itself (`--triple x86_64-apple-macosx14.2`) and joined with `lipo`.
  - The packaged app is signed with the Developer ID and the hardened runtime (entitlement: audio input). It has the same bundle identifier as the repo's build and a different signature, so macOS asks for the microphone and system audio again, and whichever of the two was allowed last is the one that works. Do not run the packaged app's audio probes here without the user: the permission questions need a person.
  - The brain's program is looked for on PATH, in the usual places, where the Node installers put commands, and last through the person's login shell (`ClaudeCodeBrain.executable`). The last two have not found anything on a real Mac yet: on this one the first place has it.
  - Notarizing sends the app to Apple under the user's developer account. Do it only when they have said yes (`package.sh --notarize`).
  - Stand notarizes with a saved Apple login of its own: a notarytool profile called `stand-notary` (`STAND_NOTARY_PROFILE` names another). On 2026-11-12 the user chose not to reuse the one Noteling's release script uses (`familiar-notary`), so never fall back to it. Only the user can make the profile: it takes an app-specific password, typed at notarytool's own prompt. They made it on 2026-11-14 (Apple first answered 403, "a required agreement is missing or has expired", until an agreement on the developer account had been accepted). On a Mac without it, `--notarize` stops before building and prints the two steps.
  - The first published build was made on 2026-11-14: `package.sh --release`, version 0.1.0, from commit `eca21a0`. Apple accepted the app and the disk image within a few minutes each. To check such a build without a person: `spctl -a -vv` on the app and `spctl -a -t open --context context:primary-signature -vv` on the image should both say "Notarized Developer ID", and `syspolicy_check distribution` should pass. - What sessions cost, learned the hard way on 2026-11-12, when $20 of prepaid Gemini credit ran out mid-afternoon and every session began to fail ("Your prepayment credits are depleted"):
  - The service bills every turn of the model for its whole context: the instructions as text, and all the speech of the session so far as audio, 25 tokens a second. There is no discount for what it has seen before. A turn is every answer, every "carry on", every note, every `nothing_to_say`.
  - So cost goes with turns times context, not with minutes: 0.2 cents a turn for the instructions, plus 0.45 cents for each minute of speech already in the session. A north-star run is about $0.21, a run of `discussion.json` $0.10, the twelve-minute meeting $0.72 (53 turns, 42 of them a decision to say nothing). An hour of steady talk would be about $5 to $10 as things are, projected.
  - The sessions used to estimate themselves by the minute and came out four times too low ($5.57 for what cost $20). `Spend` now rebuilds each session turn by turn; over the same sessions that gives $21.29.
  - `... --probe spend` prints what the kept sessions cost, by kind of run. Before a batch of runs, read it, multiply, and tell the user what the batch will cost. An afternoon of 158 runs was $11.
  - The service's own counts arrive as `usageMetadata`, one for each turn the model completes, and are recorded (`usage` events). A session that has them is priced from them; an older one is rebuilt from its events. What they show: the instructions with the tools are about 3,800 text tokens; saying nothing (`nothing_to_say`) is 8 audio tokens; the model thinks for 40 to 150 tokens before each turn; a turn that someone's speech cancels before the model produced anything is not counted.
  - The voice model keeps its instructions and at least the last three minutes of sound (`rememberMinutes`, the user's choice on 2026-11-13; `Settings.memory` turns it into the service's limit), because every turn pays for all it keeps: about 1,500 tokens for a minute of speech. `contextLimit` and `contextKeeps` (`--context-tokens`, `--context-keeps`, `STAND_CONTEXT_TOKENS`) set exact numbers for a test, and `STAND_CONTEXT_TOKENS=0` is the old behaviour of keeping everything. Do not leave it barely more than the instructions: at 4,000 in all, the question went out of mind between a lookup and its answer. The service cuts late: set to 6,500, it let sessions reach 8,000 and 8,450 before cutting back to 5,300 and 5,800, some 30 to 45 seconds after the limit was passed. So the limit is put just above what is to be kept.
  - Stand takes a turn after every sentence that is followed by 0.7 seconds of quiet. Giving it fewer turns (a longer pause when it has no part in the exchange) was tried for an evening and cost it its hearing; see above. What is left to cut is what each turn pays for: the instructions (about 4,000 tokens of text on every turn), the prompter's sheets, which pile up in the context until the oldest part is dropped, and how much speech the model keeps.
  - In a talk the prompter is Stand's memory of what passed with the audience, and nothing else (`prompts/brain-prompter-talk.md`, with the plan of the talk in it; `BrainFactory.makePrompter(root:talk:)`). The place in the talk is the stage manager's to keep, not its: see "A talk" below. It is given what the audience has heard: Stand's side from `spokenPieces`, each at the moment its sound began, because the model's transcript of itself runs far ahead of the sound, and the audience's side from the transcript. It is asked every 300 characters or so, while Stand is speaking. `talk-memory.json` is the test: with the voice model holding about a minute, Stand named the person, the team and the question it owed in 2 runs of 2, and without the prompter said "I don't have a record of your name or team" ten seconds after its memory was cut.
  - In a talk the sheet must never be passed in the moment after someone has spoken. Sent as a question ended, it took the turn in which Stand should have answered: 26 seconds of silence in the first north-star run with it. It goes when Stand's own speech has run out and nobody has spoken since (just ahead of "carry on"), or after six seconds of quiet. A discussion still passes the sheet 0.6 seconds after the last voice, as it has since it was built; nothing wrong has been seen there, and nobody has looked for it.
  - Because the model keeps so little, the prompter keeps the thread of a discussion on its sheet. `discussion-memory.json` is the test, run with `--context-tokens 6500 --context-keeps 5000` so that the start of the meeting is certainly gone from the model; `--no-prompter` shows what Stand forgets without it.
  - While an utterance is open the service does not let Stand speak. Anything that needs Stand to act when the room has just gone quiet (a note, a lookup's answer) must end the open utterance first (`endOpenUtterance`).
  - Ending an utterance and sending a note in the same instant gives the model one turn for both, and it sometimes answers the utterance with silence and drops the note. The note waits for that turn to finish (`notesHeldUntil`).
  - In a discussion, text slipped to the model while other people are talking (`inform`) is taken in and then not acted on once they stop, because they were not talking to it. Notes and answers wait for a pause there. In a talk, where the person speaking is asking Stand something, `inform` is still right.
  - Stand calls `ask_my_human` without first saying anything about one time in six. The session knows whether it has spoken this turn (`spokeThisTurn`) and, if not, answers the tool call with an instruction to say so now.
